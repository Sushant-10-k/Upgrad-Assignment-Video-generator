The water cycle is nature's way of continuously moving water around Earth.
Water from oceans and lakes evaporates due to solar energy, rising as water vapor into the atmosphere.
As it rises, it cools and condenses to form clouds through a process called condensation.
When water droplets in clouds combine and grow heavy enough, precipitation occurs as rain or snow.
On the ground, water either flows as surface runoff into rivers and streams, or infiltrates the soil.
From rivers, water eventually returns to the ocean, completing one full cycle.
The average water molecule completes this cycle approximately every 9 days in the atmosphere.
A single water molecule may stay in the ocean for up to 3,000 years before evaporating.
The water cycle drives weather patterns, distributes heat, and supports all life on Earth.
